Review checklist: your canonical sources are the tests document and the IO contract; everything else inlined below is evidence about one run, and the runner-output contract states what that evidence is obliged to be. (a) The verdict document is HOST-AUTHORED and deterministic — the per-test predicates were already evaluated against the diagnostics, and a predicate failure would have ended the phase before you were launched. Read it as the result you are reviewing; never re-derive it, never report a finding that merely restates it, and never ask for it to be changed. (b) Reconcile the diagnostics document against the raw evidence excerpt: the excerpt is a host-computed summary of the run's raw primary evidence (per-array shape, extent, counts of non-finite values, whether an array is entirely zero) and it is the ONLY view of that evidence you have — it is not the diagnostics restated, so a metric in the diagnostics that the excerpt cannot support, or a value outside the extent its array actually holds, is a fabricated or untrustworthy diagnostics: fail with `attribution=code`, or `attribution=evidence` when the raw evidence itself is what is missing or malformed. **An `all_zero` array is NOT by itself such a finding**: a flat bed, a zero transverse momentum component, a zero-diffusivity case and a lake-at-rest source term are all legitimately zero everywhere, they are common in this repository's specs, and the excerpt cannot distinguish them from an unwritten array — `min` and `max` are `0.0` for both. Treat `all_zero`, `nan_count`, `inf_count` and `shape` as FACTS to weigh against what the tests document and the IO contract say that variable should do; report one as a finding only when they say it should have varied. (c) The excerpt's coverage section carries the expected (test_id, case_id) evidence matrix against the produced one, its per-entry shortage of a test's declared raw variables, and any problem the host hit reading the evidence at all; a missing row, a whole-suite-only summary, or a missing declared variable is a fail with `attribution=code`. Its `required_evidence` section is the IO contract's own declaration against what the run actually produced — check the composition against THAT, and do not impose a fixed set of artifacts of your own. (d) Confirm the physics semantically satisfies the tests document's INTENT beyond what a mechanical predicate can express: that a pass is not a degenerate or trivial artifact, and that invariants stated in prose actually hold. For an `infrastructure` harness node, "the physics" is the runner-plumbing behaviour — emitter round-trips, case fan-out, the input guard on the case selection argument — so confirm THAT satisfies the tests document, and do NOT fail such a node for having no physics. (e) Attribution decides where the workflow returns: `code` for a defect in the generated program, `ir` for one in the intermediate representation, `evidence` for raw evidence that is missing or malformed, and `spec` for a defect in the human-authored specification — `spec` terminalizes the run for an operator rather than retrying, so use it only when no downstream repair could fix what you found. (f) Two limits are absolute: you do not author or repair anything, so report what is wrong and why rather than emitting a correction; and you never run a validator or a gate, because the deterministic gates around you have already run and are the authority for what they check.
